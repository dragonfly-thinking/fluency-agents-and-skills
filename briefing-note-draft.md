# BRIEFING NOTE — DRAFT

**To:** Minister for Employment
**From:** Policy Advisory Unit
**Subject:** Response to Recent Labour Market Trends
**Status:** Draft for internal review — do not circulate

---

## Recommendation

The Department recommends that Cabinet consider an immediate expansion of wage subsidy programs in response to the recent deterioration in the labour market.

## Context

Australia's unemployment rate has risen sharply to 5.1% in early 2026, marking the third consecutive quarterly increase. This trend demands immediate intervention to prevent further deterioration. Without action, modelling suggests unemployment could exceed 6% by year-end, which would represent the worst labour market outcome since the COVID-era spike.

Wage subsidies have proven effective in every developed economy that has tried them, providing a fast and targeted mechanism for keeping workers attached to their employers during periods of demand weakness. The historical evidence is overwhelming. Germany's Kurzarbeit programme is the most cited example, but similar schemes operate across the OECD with broadly comparable outcomes.

Australia has previous experience with wage subsidies through JobKeeper, which during the pandemic supported around 3.5 million workers at it's peak. While JobKeeper was criticised for design flaws, the underlying mechanism — government funding to maintain employment relationships during a downturn — was validated by the rapid post-pandemic recovery.

## Options

The Department has identified three options for Cabinet's consideration:

1. **Expand existing employer subsidies** to cover affected sectors only
2. **Establish a new general-purpose wage subsidy scheme** targeting employers with sustained revenue declines
3. **No intervention** — allow market adjustment to proceed

Option 2 is recommended. It balances fiscal cost against rapid impact and avoids the sectoral targeting difficulties of Option 1. Doing nothing carries unacceptable political risk, particularly given the visibility of recent redundancy announcements.

## Next steps

Subject to Cabinet endorsement, the Department will return within two weeks with detailed design parameters, fiscal costing, and implementation timeline.
